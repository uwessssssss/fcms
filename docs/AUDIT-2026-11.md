# Security and accounting audit, November 2026

Scope: the database (65 → 68 migrations), the API's authorisation and SQL, and
the books under randomised trading. Each finding marked **proven** has a test
that succeeded as an attack on the code before the fix and is refused after it.

How to rerun everything: `./check-all.sh` (local) or the `ci` workflow (GitHub).
The attacks alone: `psql -d fcms -f db/test/security.sql` after the seeds.

## Findings

| Id | Severity | Finding | Fix | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | High | `fn_gl_reverse` reversed **any** journal of **any** company for any caller: no company check, no role check. The API allowed only accountants and head office; the database did not | Reversal by accountant or head office of the same company only; triggers use `fn_gl_reverse_internal` | Fixed, proven (650) |
| S2 | High | `fn_bank_post_statement_line` posted another company's bank statement line into the books | The line must be the actor's company's | Fixed, proven (650) |
| S3 | High | Every ledger action trusted the `p_actor` it was given: a supervisor could post a manual journal or close a month in head office's name; another company could close yours with one of your staff ids | `fn_ledger_actor`: inside a request the actor must be the signed-in user (`app.staff_id`) and of the session's company | Fixed, proven (650) |
| S4 | High | A journal or line could carry another company's site, customer, supplier, staff, tank, item, channel, product or money account | Checked on every journal and line insert | Fixed, proven (650) |
| S5 | Medium | The app role could call the posting machinery directly: the backfill (every company's records), re-seeding any company's chart, posting any record | Execute revoked; the triggers that drive posting run as the ledger owner | Fixed, proven (650) |
| S6 | Medium | Imported bank statements and recorded money transfers could be edited after the fact (080 grants UPDATE on every table); an account's code or type could change after it had postings | Append-only triggers; an account keeps its code, type and control once used | Fixed, proven (650) |
| A1 | Medium | A station **manager** could see every company bank and wallet balance, open accounts, move money between company accounts and import or match the company's statements (bank reconciliation, 630, used permissions a station manager holds). The books already limited the same manager to their own station | Company accounts belong to head office; station accounts to their station (writes by trigger, reads filtered by the API) | Fixed, proven (660) |
| R1 | Medium | Wallet reconciliation matched one payment to one book line by amount and day **before** it used shift windows. Two customers paying the same amount on one day were then mixed up: a shift whose whole MoMo total equalled one payment took another shift's, and both shifts stayed open (seed 97: 41 statement lines and 3 book items open on Airtel; seed 555: 33 and 3). The default seed never hit it | Wallets match by shift window first, then one-to-one, then by day | Fixed, proven (670): the same seeds now reconcile to nothing open; the pre-audit code fails them with identical numbers |
| T1 | Test | The simulator demanded zero stock warnings from dip noise, which is statistically wrong over a long run (noise crosses the 10 L floor at about 3.1σ), so its result depended on the seed (1234 raised one at 10.5 L) | Allows the expected handful, and fails on any warning larger than noise can produce (book stock wrong) | Fixed (simulate.py) |
| A2 | Low | No fallback authorisation policy: every endpoint asks for sign-in today, but one added later without `RequireAuthorization()` would have been open | Sign-in required by default; login and `/health` marked anonymous | Fixed (Program.cs) — compiled only in CI |

Corrections to first readings, stated so nobody repeats them: the movement
tables (`fuel_movement`, `dry_movement`, `money_movement`) and `audit_log` were
already append-only by `DO INSTEAD NOTHING` rules. An update "succeeds" with
zero rows; the probe recognises that as blocked.

## Looked at and left as they are

| Item | Why it stays |
|---|---|
| SQL injection | None found: every query in the API is parameterised (Dapper); no SQL is built from strings |
| Row-level security | Every table with a `tenant_id` has RLS enabled, forced, and a policy |
| JWT signing key | Required, minimum 32 bytes, the template's placeholder deliberately too short to start with; issuer, audience and lifetime validated |
| Sign-in | One message for unknown user and wrong password; rate-limited; lockout |
| CORS allows any `localhost` origin | Tokens live in `sessionStorage`, not cookies, so another local site cannot use them. Reasoned in Program.cs |
| `fn_auth_diagnose` (says why a sign-in failed) | Used only for the server log; the reply stays vague |
| Swagger UI in every environment | Shows the API's shape, not data. Turn it off behind the proxy if that matters to you |
| Silent `DO INSTEAD NOTHING` rules | Safe, but an update that does nothing reports success. A trigger that raises would be louder; not changed in this audit |

## The books under randomised trading

The simulator trades real days through the API's own database role: shifts,
meter and dip readings, credit sales at agreed prices, cash drops, deliveries,
supplier invoices and payments, banking, mobile money and its statements, with
planted problems the reconciliation must find. After each run every control
must agree, every journal balance, every closed shift be in the books, and a
year-end close (tried, rolled back) must move the year's profit to retained
earnings to the shilling.

Seven months of trading, each with its own random seed (`SIM_SEED=n tools/sim/run.sh days`):

| Seed | Days | Shifts | Year-end profit to 3100 (UGX) | Detected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 21 | 42 | 19,865,772 | 0 of 0 losses, 4 of 4 shortages, 4 over-cap (at most 5) |
| 31 | 21 | 42 | 20,039,222 | 1 of 1 losses, 5 of 5 shortages, 3 over-cap (at most 5) |
| 97 | 30 | 60 | 25,272,838 | 2 of 2 losses, 9 of 9 shortages, 7 over-cap (at most 7) |
| 1234 | 30 | 60 | 24,857,317 | 0 of 0 losses, 2 of 2 shortages, 6 over-cap; 1 noise warning (10.5 L) |
| 555 | 60 | 120 | 50,806,017 | 5 of 5 losses, 10 of 10 shortages, 10 over-cap (at most 14) |
| 42 | 30 | 60 | 25,434,337 | 0 of 0 losses, 10 of 10 shortages, 5 over-cap (at most 7) |
| 888 | 45 | 90 | 44,091,216 | 3 of 3 losses, 3 of 3 shortages, 11 over-cap (at most 14) |

In every run: every control agreed with its record, every journal balanced,
every closed shift was in the books once, the bank showed exactly the two
planted problems, both wallets reconciled to nothing open, and the year close
moved the profit to retained earnings to the shilling. Before 670, seeds 97 and
555 failed on the Airtel wallet (R1).

## What this audit could not run

The sandbox had no npm registry and no .NET SDK, so the web install, type
check and production build, and the API build, ran only in CI
(`.github/workflows/ci.yml`). The C# changes are small (two SQL filters, the
fallback policy, two `AllowAnonymous` calls); the SQL in them was prepared
against the database here.
